## LIT-001

source: arXiv:2205.03690, fetched 2026-08-14
authors: A. Amice, H. Dai, P. Werner, A. Zhang, R. Tedrake
result: C-Iris algorithm (C-space Iterative Regional Inflation by Semidefinite
  programming), full paper — "Finding and Optimizing Certified, Collision-Free
  Regions in Configuration Space for Robot Manipulators"

statement: C-Iris generates large, convex polytopes in a rational parametrization of
  configuration space that are guaranteed collision-free, by alternating between
  (1) a simultaneous search for a maximal-volume ellipse inscribed in a given
  polytope together with a certificate that the polytope is collision-free, and
  (2) a maximal expansion of the polytope away from that ellipse that does not
  violate the certificate. Volume and polytope size grow over iterations while
  remaining collision-free by construction.

hypotheses:
  - obstacles in task space are convex
  - configuration space may be arbitrary-dimensional
  - configuration space admits a rational parametrization (the paper's Introduction
    and Method sections use this to convert the certificate search into
    semidefinite/convex optimization)

translation: RoboCert's tangent-half-angle rationalization (`AGENTS.md` §7.2,
  `README.md` §3) produces exactly the "rational parametrization of C-space"
  hypothesis this result needs, for revolute-jointed manipulators. RoboCert's
  §8.3 ("Convex separation certificates") and §9 (collision certification levels
  A–C, i.e. analytic convex primitives / certified convex decomposition / algebraic
  surface models) already require task-space geometry to be convex or convexly
  decomposed before this class of certificate applies — the hypothesis match is by
  design, not coincidence, since C-Iris is one of RoboCert's stated research
  foundations (`README.md` §33).

gap: C-Iris's guarantee is about a *given* polytope/ellipse pair discovered by its
  own alternating optimization — the optimization itself is a candidate generator,
  not part of the certificate. Any RoboCert use of this result must keep the
  generated polytope/ellipse data as the certificate payload and re-check the
  underlying SOS/SDP feasibility independently (`AGENTS.md` §9.1 "Numerical SDP
  output is not yet a certificate") rather than trusting the solver's convergence.
  No gap has been identified in the convexity/rationality hypotheses themselves for
  the planar/spatial manipulator families RoboCert currently targets (Phases 1–4);
  Level D (general CAD/B-Rep, `README.md` §9) geometry is out of scope for this
  result until converted to Level A–C representations.
