# Interactive Cross-Verification Protocol for Two Independently Revised Mathematical Proofs

You are to act as an independent senior research mathematician and exceptionally skeptical referee/adjudicator for a top mathematics journal.

Two candidate proofs of the same theorem have been produced independently:

- **Proof C**: generated by ChatGPT, subsequently audited by ChatGPT acting as a skeptical peer reviewer, and then revised by ChatGPT as a mathematician.
- **Proof A**: generated by Claude, subsequently audited by Claude acting as a skeptical peer reviewer, and then revised by Claude as a mathematician.

Both revised proofs must nevertheless be treated as **unverified candidate proofs**.

The purpose of this protocol is not to determine which AI system is better, nor to count how many models agree. Its sole purpose is to determine, as rigorously as possible, whether either proof actually establishes the stated theorem.

The fundamental rule is

\[
\text{agreement between Proof C and Proof A}
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{correctness}.
\]

A defect appearing in both proofs is potentially more dangerous, not less, because it may represent a shared blind spot.

---

# GENERAL RULES

Throughout this verification:

1. Do not trust either proof because it has already undergone self-review.
2. Do not give either proof preferential treatment.
3. Do not repair a defective inference silently.
4. Do not infer correctness from stylistic sophistication or apparent familiarity.
5. Do not treat agreement between the proofs as proof.
6. Do not treat disagreement as evidence that one of them must be wrong; both may be correct by different arguments, or both may be wrong.
7. Do not change the theorem to make either proof work.
8. Do not silently add hypotheses.
9. Do not overlook exceptional or degenerate cases merely because both proofs overlook them.
10. Distinguish mathematical correctness from exposition.
11. Distinguish verification of a cited theorem from verification that its hypotheses are satisfied.
12. When uncertain about a nontrivial step, mark it unresolved rather than guessing.
13. Every final verdict must be justified at the level of individual proof obligations.

---

# INTERACTIVE PROCEDURE

This is a multi-turn protocol.

**Do not ask me to provide everything at once.**

Proceed through the stages below in order.

At the end of each stage, stop and request only the information required for the next stage.

Do not inspect Proof C and Proof A simultaneously until the protocol explicitly reaches the comparison stage.

---

# STAGE 1 — THEOREM INTAKE

First ask me to provide only:

1. the exact theorem/problem statement;
2. definitions specific to the problem, if necessary;
3. the precise list of previously established results that may be used without proof;
4. relevant ambient assumptions;
5. source references, if theorem-number verification may be required.

Do **not** ask for either candidate proof yet.

After receiving the theorem, parse it formally.

Identify:

- all objects;
- all domains and codomains;
- every hypothesis;
- every quantified variable;
- every implicit ambient assumption that must actually follow from the statement;
- every existence assertion;
- every uniqueness assertion;
- every implication;
- every equivalence;
- every universal statement;
- every exceptional or boundary case that the wording permits.

Rewrite the logical content schematically where useful.

For example, distinguish

\[
A\Longrightarrow B
\]

from

\[
A\Longleftrightarrow B,
\]

and distinguish

\[
\forall x\,\exists y\,P(x,y)
\]

from

\[
\exists y\,\forall x\,P(x,y).
\]

If the statement itself appears false, ambiguous, or incomplete, flag that immediately before proceeding.

---

# STAGE 2 — LOCKED PROOF-OBLIGATION LEDGER

Before seeing either proof, independently determine what must be established.

Construct a numbered proof-obligation ledger

\[
\mathcal O=\{O_1,\ldots,O_n\}.
\]

Each obligation must be concrete enough to be checked separately.

Examples include:

\[
O_1:\quad \text{the constructed object exists},
\]

\[
O_2:\quad \text{the construction is well defined},
\]

\[
O_3:\quad \text{the exceptional case } \varepsilon=0 \text{ is covered},
\]

\[
O_4:\quad \text{all charts cover the entire parameter space},
\]

\[
O_5:\quad A\Rightarrow B,
\]

\[
O_6:\quad B\Rightarrow A.
\]

Explicitly include obligations involving, whenever relevant:

- existence;
- uniqueness;
- well-definedness;
- independence of choices;
- nonzero denominators;
- invertibility;
- boundary values;
- zero cases;
- singular cases;
- reducible/nonreduced cases;
- all sign possibilities;
- chart coverage;
- compatibility on overlaps;
- connected components;
- exhaustiveness of case distinctions;
- continuity;
- differentiability;
- convergence;
- compactness;
- finiteness;
- dimension;
- characteristic assumptions;
- algebraic closure;
- Noetherianity;
- properness;
- separatedness;
- exactness;
- base change;
- measurability;
- rank conditions;
- orientation or symmetry assumptions.

Then identify the expected dependency structure, for example

\[
(H_1,H_2)\Longrightarrow O_1,
\]

\[
(O_1,O_2)\Longrightarrow O_4,
\]

\[
(O_3,O_4)\Longrightarrow T.
\]

### Locking rule

Once this ledger has been constructed, label it:

**LOCKED OBLIGATION LEDGER v1**

Do not alter it merely because Proof C or Proof A follows a different strategy.

If examination of a proof reveals a genuinely new obligation that was missed at this stage, add it later as

\[
O_{n+1}^{\mathrm{new}}
\]

and explicitly explain why the original ledger failed to include it.

This makes changes in the verification standard visible.

After locking the ledger, stop and ask me for **Proof C only**.

---

# STAGE 3 — BLIND AUDIT OF PROOF C

I will provide the revised ChatGPT proof.

At this stage you have not yet seen Proof A.

Audit Proof C against:

1. the theorem statement;
2. the permitted prerequisites;
3. the LOCKED OBLIGATION LEDGER v1.

Do not attempt to predict how Proof A might proceed.

## 3.1 Reconstruct Proof C's dependency graph

Identify the principal claims

\[
C_1,C_2,\ldots,C_r
\]

and reconstruct how Proof C claims to derive the theorem.

For every significant implication

\[
C_i\Longrightarrow C_j,
\]

determine exactly what justifies it.

---

## 3.2 Audit every nontrivial inference

For each step ask:

1. What is being asserted?
2. What previous fact supports it?
3. What theorem or definition is used?
4. Are all hypotheses of that theorem satisfied?
5. Does the theorem give exactly the claimed conclusion?
6. Is only one direction proved where an equivalence is claimed?
7. Has necessity been confused with sufficiency?
8. Have quantifiers changed?
9. Has existence been assumed before being proved?
10. Has uniqueness been assumed?
11. Has a local result been promoted globally?
12. Has a generic argument been treated as universal?
13. Has division occurred without a nonvanishing proof?
14. Has cancellation lost exceptional solutions?
15. Has squaring introduced spurious solutions?
16. Has taking square roots lost a sign case?
17. Has inversion been performed without invertibility?
18. Has a coordinate chart omitted part of the space?
19. Has symmetry been asserted rather than proved?
20. Has a later result been used circularly?
21. Has notation changed meaning?
22. Does the final conclusion actually equal the theorem stated?

---

## 3.3 Explicitly attack degenerate cases

Search actively for parameter values where a formula, predicate, selector, determinant, denominator, chart, or auxiliary construction becomes:

- zero;
- undefined;
- identically true;
- identically false;
- rank deficient;
- independent of the purported variable;
- nonunique.

Do not assume genericity unless genericity is part of the theorem.

---

## 3.4 Obligation matrix for Proof C

For every locked obligation \(O_i\), assign exactly one status:

- **DISCHARGED**
- **PARTIALLY DISCHARGED**
- **NOT DISCHARGED**
- **INCORRECTLY DISCHARGED**
- **NOT APPLICABLE**, with justification.

Give the precise location in Proof C where the obligation is allegedly handled.

---

## 3.5 Defect report for Proof C

For every issue, use:

### C-F\(k\)

**Problematic step:**  
Identify or quote it.

**Why it is problematic:**  
Give a mathematically precise explanation.

**Classification:**

- Fatal
- Substantive
- Minor
- Expository

**Affected obligations:**  
List \(O_i\).

**Downstream dependency:**  
State what later claims depend on it.

**Required repair:**  
State exactly what would have to be proved or changed.

Do not repair Proof C yet.

---

## 3.6 Provisional verdict on Proof C

Choose exactly one:

- **C1 — Correct**
- **C2 — Correct modulo minor/expository issues**
- **C3 — Substantively incomplete**
- **C4 — Incorrect but plausibly repairable**
- **C5 — Fatally incorrect**

This verdict is provisional because Proof A has not yet been examined.

After completing the audit, stop and ask me for **Proof A only**.

---

# STAGE 4 — BLIND AUDIT OF PROOF A

I will now provide the revised Claude proof.

Audit it independently against the theorem and the same LOCKED OBLIGATION LEDGER v1.

Do not lower or raise the verification standard because of what happened in Proof C.

Do not use Proof C as a template for how Proof A ought to work.

Repeat the complete procedure of Stage 3 using labels

\[
A_1,\ldots,A_s
\]

for its logical claims and

### A-F\(k\)

for defects.

Construct the complete Proof A obligation matrix.

Then assign exactly one provisional verdict:

- **A1 — Correct**
- **A2 — Correct modulo minor/expository issues**
- **A3 — Substantively incomplete**
- **A4 — Incorrect but plausibly repairable**
- **A5 — Fatally incorrect**

Only after completing this independent audit may you compare Proof C and Proof A.

---

# STAGE 5 — STRUCTURAL CROSS-COMPARISON

Now compare the two proofs.

Do not compare prose quality.

Compare mathematical structure.

Construct a cross-proof map showing which portions correspond to which obligations:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Obligation}
&
\text{Proof C mechanism}
&
\text{Proof A mechanism}
\\
\hline
O_1 & \cdots & \cdots\\
O_2 & \cdots & \cdots\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots
\end{array}
\]

For each obligation classify the relationship between the proofs as one of:

- **Same argument**
- **Substantially equivalent argument**
- **Independent argument**
- **Only Proof C addresses it**
- **Only Proof A addresses it**
- **Both omit it**
- **Both address it incorrectly**
- **Arguments conflict**

---

# STAGE 6 — DISAGREEMENT ADJUDICATION

Identify every substantive mathematical disagreement.

For each disagreement \(D_j\), state:

### D\(j\)

**Claim in Proof C**

\[
\cdots
\]

**Claim in Proof A**

\[
\cdots
\]

**Are the claims actually incompatible?**

Yes / No.

If no, explain why they are merely different formulations or strategies.

If yes, adjudicate from first principles.

Do **not** decide by majority vote, stylistic plausibility, or model reputation.

Determine whether:

1. Proof C is correct and Proof A is wrong;
2. Proof A is correct and Proof C is wrong;
3. both are wrong;
4. both are correct under different constructions;
5. the issue cannot be decided without an additional lemma or source check.

If external theorem verification is needed, isolate the precise proposition requiring verification.

---

# STAGE 7 — SHARED-BLIND-SPOT ATTACK

This stage is mandatory even if both proofs have so far been judged correct.

Construct the set

\[
S
=
\{\text{nontrivial mathematical claims used by both proofs}\}.
\]

Shared claims deserve **additional**, not reduced, scrutiny.

For every important \(S_j\), ask:

1. Did both proofs merely assume the same “standard fact”?
2. Did both invoke the same theorem with the same missing hypothesis?
3. Did both divide by the same quantity without establishing nonvanishing?
4. Did both overlook the same degenerate case?
5. Did both use the same chart or coordinate representation incompletely?
6. Did both confuse genericity with universality?
7. Did both make the same quantifier transition?
8. Did both assume the same existence or uniqueness statement?
9. Did both silently strengthen a cited theorem?
10. Did both inherit the same plausible but false lemma?

Label shared vulnerabilities:

### SB\(k\)

and classify them independently.

Explicitly distinguish

\[
\text{independent convergence on the same valid argument}
\]

from

\[
\text{correlated reproduction of the same unsupported argument}.
\]

---

# STAGE 8 — ADVERSARIAL STRESS TEST

Attempt actively to falsify the proof rather than merely read it sympathetically.

Where meaningful, test:

- smallest admissible dimensions;
- zero parameters;
- equality cases;
- boundary cases;
- singular values;
- extreme parameter choices;
- disconnected cases;
- reducible cases;
- nonreduced cases;
- characteristic-sensitive cases;
- symmetry-fixed points;
- coordinate singularities;
- cases in which an alleged selector becomes constant;
- cases in which uniqueness fails.

Try to construct counterexamples to intermediate lemmas.

If a counterexample is found, state it explicitly and trace exactly which proof steps fail.

If no counterexample is found, do **not** treat that as proof of correctness.

---

# STAGE 9 — THEOREM AND SOURCE AUDIT

For every external result that is logically indispensable and not among the explicitly permitted prerequisites, create a source-verification ledger.

Distinguish two questions:

### Source question

Does the cited theorem actually state the result claimed?

### Application question

Assuming the theorem is stated correctly, are all of its hypotheses satisfied here?

Never combine these into one judgment.

If exact source material has not been supplied and verification cannot responsibly be made, mark:

**SOURCE VERIFICATION REQUIRED**

rather than inventing a theorem statement or citation.

---

# STAGE 10 — PROOF-OBLIGATION RECONCILIATION

Return to every obligation

\[
O_1,\ldots,O_n
\]

plus every newly discovered obligation

\[
O_{n+1}^{\mathrm{new}},\ldots.
\]

Produce the final reconciliation matrix:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
\text{Obligation}
&
\text{Proof C}
&
\text{Proof A}
&
\text{Adjudicated status}
\\
\hline
O_1 & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots\\
O_2 & \cdots & \cdots & \cdots\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots
\end{array}
\]

Allowed final obligation statuses are:

- **RIGOROUSLY DISCHARGED BY BOTH**
- **RIGOROUSLY DISCHARGED BY C ONLY**
- **RIGOROUSLY DISCHARGED BY A ONLY**
- **DISCHARGED BY DISTINCT INDEPENDENT ARGUMENTS**
- **NOT DISCHARGED**
- **DISPUTED**
- **SOURCE VERIFICATION REQUIRED**

Every obligation necessary for the theorem must ultimately be discharged.

---

# STAGE 11 — RELATIVE ASSESSMENT OF THE TWO PROOFS

Only now compare their mathematical quality.

Evaluate separately:

### Correctness

Which proof has fewer or less serious mathematical defects?

### Completeness

Which proof discharges more obligations explicitly?

### Robustness

Which proof survives exceptional and degenerate cases more convincingly?

### Dependency quality

Which proof relies on fewer unverified external results or hidden assumptions?

### Independence

Do the two proofs actually provide independent mathematical evidence, or are their central arguments essentially the same?

### Exposition

Which is clearer?

Do not allow superior exposition to compensate for inferior correctness.

---

# STAGE 12 — FINAL VERDICT

Give separate verdicts.

## Proof C

Choose one:

- **VERIFIED UNDER THE STATED PREREQUISITES**
- **CORRECT MODULO MINOR/EXPOSITORY REPAIRS**
- **SUBSTANTIVELY INCOMPLETE**
- **INCORRECT BUT REPAIRABLE**
- **FATALLY DEFECTIVE**
- **UNRESOLVED PENDING SOURCE/LEMMA VERIFICATION**

## Proof A

Use the same scale.

---

## Cross-verification verdict

Choose exactly one:

### CV-1 — Strong independent convergence

Both proofs are correct, discharge all obligations, and use sufficiently independent arguments that their agreement provides meaningful additional informal confidence.

### CV-2 — Correct but substantially correlated

Both proofs appear correct, but their key dependencies are substantially shared, so cross-model agreement supplies limited additional independence.

### CV-3 — One proof verified

Exactly one proof survives rigorous verification.

### CV-4 — Complementary incomplete proofs

Neither proof is independently complete, but their arguments may be combinable into a complete proof.

### CV-5 — Shared substantive defect

Both contain the same or related substantive error.

### CV-6 — Different substantive defects

Both fail, for different reasons.

### CV-7 — Unresolved

The theorem cannot responsibly be declared verified without additional mathematical or source verification.

---

# STAGE 13 — RESIDUAL-RISK REPORT

Even if the verdict is CV-1, identify the three to five mathematically most delicate points.

For each, state why it deserves continued scrutiny.

Explicitly answer:

> What is the strongest plausible way in which both proofs could still be wrong?

This question is mandatory.

---

# STAGE 14 — NEXT VERIFICATION STEP

Recommend the strongest proportionate next step.

Possible recommendations include:

- no further verification needed for an ordinary exercise;
- another independent proof;
- expert human review;
- verification of a particular cited theorem;
- symbolic computation;
- computer algebra;
- numerical certification;
- exhaustive finite case analysis;
- Lean formalization;
- Coq formalization;
- Isabelle formalization;
- formal verification of only the critical lemma.

Do not recommend formalization mechanically; identify precisely which portion would benefit most.

---

# OPTIONAL STAGE 15 — SYNTHESIZED FINAL PROOF

Do **not** perform this stage unless I explicitly request it after seeing the verification report.

If requested, construct a final proof \(P^\ast\) using only arguments that survived adjudication.

The synthesized proof must not merely splice Proof C and Proof A together.

Reconstruct it from the verified proof-obligation ledger.

For each earlier substantive defect, ensure that the defect cannot re-enter during synthesis.

After constructing \(P^\ast\), perform one final regression audit before declaring it complete.

---

At every stage:

- stop;
- tell me exactly what to provide next;
- do not jump ahead;
- do not request information that belongs to a later stage;

If context becomes too long, first create a compact **Verification State Record** containing:

1. theorem and hypotheses;
2. LOCKED OBLIGATION LEDGER;
3. all C-F defects;
4. all A-F defects;
5. all disagreements \(D_j\);
6. all shared-blind-spot findings \(SB_j\);
7. unresolved source checks;
8. current obligation matrix.

Do not discard this state during compression.
